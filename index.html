    The Method

    Okay, but how is it in the know?

    I turned Austin into a graph, gave every place its own rules, and taught the planner how a girl actually spends a Saturday. It keeps track of the time, the neighborhood, the drive, when you last ate, the weather and how much energy you have left. It knows what belongs before brunch and what never happens right after Pilates. Then it finds the one day that actually fits. On Saturdays, we brunch.

    Austin is dots and lines

    Every neighborhood is a dot, and every road is a line with minutes on it. To go somewhere, it adds up the minutes on each path and picks the smallest, like the fastest way through a maze. Here, 8 + 7 = 15 minutes.

    Grown-up word: Dijkstra’s shortest-path algorithm

    A lottery with extra tickets

    Every place gets lottery tickets. Places I love get more, and places far away get fewer. Then it draws about a dozen for today, so every Saturday is different but the favorites keep winning.

    Grown-up word: weighted sampling without replacement

    Rules about being a person

    The planner carries a tiny backpack of facts about you: did you just work out? Did you just eat? Every next stop has to pass the rules. Coffee in gym clothes is fine. Shopping in them is not.

    Grown-up word: a state machine (rules.step())

    Building the day, one stop at a time

    It grows every possible day one stop at a time. When two days end up in the same place, at the same time, having done the same things, it only keeps the better one. That turns billions of orders into less than a second.

    Grown-up word: bitmask dynamic programming

    Checking its homework

    A slow, careful version tries every single order. The fast planner has to get the exact same answer on dozens of made-up Saturdays, or the tests fail. It also checks the people rules one by one.

    Grown-up word: brute-force backtracking tests

    Same number, same Saturday

    The lottery is random, but every Saturday gets a number. Type the number back in, or send the letter link, and you get the exact same day, stop for stop.

    Grown-up word: a seeded random number generator
